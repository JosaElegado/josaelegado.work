Why do I work this way?

Full chapter →

A working design is roughly the halfway mark.

I trained as an engineer. Engineering teaches you that a problem is solved when the design works. A few years of fieldwork taught me that a working design is about halfway.

The pattern repeats everywhere I’ve worked — renewable energy, agriculture, the public sector. Financing that doesn’t match how people actually earn is a constraint. Institutions that change faster than a pilot can adapt to them are a constraint. Trust is a constraint, and it isn’t transferable.

none of these show up on a spec sheet

The technology is rarely the constraint.

So I stopped trying to design better solutionsconditions and started trying to understand adoption. In practice that means being on the site rather than reading the report about the site. Sitting in the meeting where the decision actually gets made. Asking the question that makes a room briefly uncomfortable, because that is usually the question with the answer in it.

And writing all of it down — because the detail that turns out to matter is almost never the one I walked in expecting to matter. That’s what the notebook is for. Not a habit. The method.

which is why every page here has a date on it

Field noteJul 2026Renewable energy

What renewable energy pilots don’t tell you

A pilot is designed to prove that something works. That makes it a poor instrument for finding out whether anyone wants it. Every pilot I’ve been part of arrived with support that would not exist afterwards — a coordinator, a subsidy, someone who answered the phone. The interesting month is not any month during the pilot. It is the first one after it ends.
